The Great Noddy Car Gamble

How a Cypriot casino owner tried to sell Vignale-Fiats to Britain — and how it all went wrong.

By Cris Rees, Thoroughbred & Classic Cars, January 1994.

In 1967, Cypriot casino owner Frixos Demetriou spotted an unusual small car at Milan airport. Within weeks he had struck a deal with Vignale: he would buy the factory’s entire production for the next six months, on condition every car was built right-hand drive for the British market.

Four models crossed the Channel: the Gamine, the 850 Special, the Eveline and the Samantha. A separate arrangement with coachbuilder Francis Lombardi added the Lombardi Grand Prix to the range. The cars launched at the Earls Court Motor Show in September 1968, priced from £700 for a Gamine up to £2,211 for a Samantha.

British buyers were unconvinced. Around 300 Gamines and 27 Samanthas found homes, with barely any Evelines sold, and quality problems — poor welding, ill-fitting trim, unreliable parts — didn’t help the cause. By 1970 Demetriou had given up on the British market and returned to Cyprus. The venture ended as a financial fiasco, though at least his gambling licence survived the ordeal.

Demetriou himself did not long survive his return: he died after a runaway army tank crushed the Vignale-Fiat he was sitting in — a story told in fuller detail in ‘The Gamine, the Gambling King and the Tank’.
