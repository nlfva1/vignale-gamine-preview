The Gamine, the Gambling King and the Tank

The fuller, stranger story of Frixos Demetriou — and how it ended.

By Andrew Adamides, Cyprus Mail, Sunday 1 March 1998.

Frixos Demetriou had already made his fortune running a casino in Cyprus and the Olympic Gaming Club in London when a change in British gaming law, around 1967, threatened to close that business down. He needed a new venture — and found one after spotting a Fiat Gamine at an Italian airport.

Demetriou pursued the marketing rights for Vignale-built cars with the same determination he’d brought to the casino floor, buying up “the factory’s entire production for the next six months” and insisting on right-hand drive for the British market. The cars were launched with fanfare at the 1968 Earls Court Motor Show, but the reception soon soured: poor welding, rust and unreliable components, combined with styling British buyers found simply too “kooky”, left around 800 cars unsold and the customs bills mounting.

By 1970, Demetriou re-exported the unsold stock to Cyprus, where the drier climate suited the cars far better than Britain’s had. He didn’t get to enjoy the outcome for long: a few months after returning home, he died when a runaway British army tank careened into the parked Vignale-Fiat he was sitting in.

The cars themselves outlasted him. Surviving Gamines — in Cyprus and well beyond — have proven remarkably durable, provided they’re kept somewhere drier than the England that first rejected them.
