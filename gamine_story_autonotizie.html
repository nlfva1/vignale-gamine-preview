c. 1968 · Vignale press bulletin

Autonotizie Vignale

A factory tour through Carrozzeria Vignale, straight from the coachbuilder’s own newsletter.

From Autonotizie, Carrozzeria Vignale’s in-house press bulletin, first published in 1963.

Around 1968, Vignale used its newsletter to invite the public inside its Turin factory, under the banner “we wish to be better known”. Visitors would pass offices for purchasing, management and sales before reaching a boardroom lined with trophies, including honours from the Mille Miglia (1951–1953) and the 1968 Mercurio d’Oro, plus a gallery of photos of clients and friends from the worlds of motorsport and art.

The factory floor was turning out several models at once: the two-seat Fiat 500 Gamine roadster (10 a day), the four-seat Fiat 850 Berlina (16 a day), the Fiat 124 Eveline (8 a day), the Fiat 125 Samantha (2 a day), and six to eight Maseratis a week — supported by press and assembly machinery, a paint shop and a chrome-plating department.

Of the Gamine itself, Vignale wrote that Comm. Vignale had set out to build a small car in 1930s style with a modern touch, on the Fiat 500 chassis. Its name — French for “little scamp” — was chosen to match. First shown in France, the car was by 1968 sold worldwide, had just won the Journalistic Rallye Monte-Carlo under drivers Bozon and Issontier and appeared at the Monaco Grand Prix, and was now also offered with an optional hard top for winter comfort.
