c. 1968 · Vignale press bulletin

Autonotizie Vignale

A factory tour through Carrozzeria Vignale, straight from the coachbuilder’s own newsletter.

From Autonotizie, Carrozzeria Vignale’s in-house press bulletin, first published in 1963.

INTRODUCTION

Our car news bulletin was issued for the first time in 1963, in a very simple guise and poor as regards information. Since that time, we have tried to improve it, doing something more, but we think we failed our object. We have the deep-rooted feeling that we have neglected many sound arguments, in such a way as to fail the main purpose of the bulletin.

To err is human, says an old proverb, and we think that time has taught us something, above all in the specific field of public relations. “We wish to be better known” is the title given to this issue of our bulletin. It is a complete programme (and a very wide one too) that we want to carry out in a clear and detailed way. We therefore invite our kind friends to dwell with us upon this friendly conversation.

Press Service and P.R. Vignale

COME IN, PLEASE

We are very happy for our friends’ visits, so we say, come in please, to all those who have not yet come to see us. Come in, please. Our factory welcomes you and we are really glad to receive and take you inside. People say that it is comfortable, but you only have the right to express an unbiased opinion about it. We can only say, by your leave, that it has been built piece by piece, lovingly and with many sacrifices…

Now you are inside, and your natural curiosity needs satisfying. On the left, as you can see, are our offices (Purchasing, Management, Sales) and upstairs, for the moment, there is a file room. Probably you are not interested in these things, so it will be better to go on. We want to show you the meeting room, where you can see the cups and prizes of our various competitions. The most desired trophy is probably the one from the ‘Mille Miglia’ of the far-off years 1951-52-53; the ‘Mercurio d’Oro 1968’ also deserves your attention. If you like, you can look at the photographs on the walls, which also contain many memories. As you can see, it is a gallery of people — customers and friends — from the world of motor-racing, the artistic world and, at last, royalty.

And now let us visit the factory itself. In 1961 it was not yet so big, and we can say we finished building it only in these very days. Here in the central shed are the assembly lines of the following cars:

  • FIAT 500 roadster model – 2 seats, ‘Gamine’, with an output of 10 units per day
  • FIAT 850 special model – Berlina, 4 seats, with 16 units per day
  • FIAT 124 ‘Eveline’ model, 4 seats, with an output of 8 cars per day
  • FIAT 125 ‘Samantha’ model, 4 seats, with 2 units per day

To this output we must add the various models we build for Maserati’s factory in Modena (4-door and Mexico 5000), which reach 6-8 cars per week.

Let us continue the visit together and we see, in front of us, the oleo-dynamic working presses. They are gigantic machines that carry out an enormous amount of work; in fact, their work produces all the forming dies for the various pressed parts used in the assembly of our models. Gone is the nostalgic era of manual labour — progress demands its share. Beside the 600-metric-ton giants, there are the mechanical presses, no less important even if lighter.

Let us take another little walk (actually not too little) and we reach the assembly of raw bodies, and it is just there that the little ‘Gamine’ is born. We are glad to see that you are interested in this visit, and we ask you to dwell upon this little car. From the moment the car frame reaches the line, after a few operations, you can see the car taking shape and coming out finished — you might not have believed it before, but now, on the contrary, you can say you have seen a miracle.

We have left the painting cycle as the last stage, as it is perhaps the most complicated and complete cycle. Rather than bore you with a description of all the operations, of which there are many (plate pickling, sound-deadening, primer spraying, body cooling, baking, stopper finishing, etc.), we leave everything to your imagination in this fanciful and unreal world. It seems, in fact, to be a kind of spatial complex, where electronic push-buttons are certainly not lacking. Here the bodies still travel along the ground, and rise into the air only at set moments.

We also have inside a small chromium-plating department, which meets over 50 per cent of our needs, but this place is kept secret, owing to the chemical processes involved. In another special department we work synthetic resin, particularly suited for the drive-shaft cover, instrument panel, armrest, etc. The actual site covers about 40,000 square metres, of which the covered surface is about 32,000 metres.

Our visit to the factory is now at an end, but we can keep talking as good friends. If you like, we can go to our in-house social club, the ‘dopolavoro’, where our workers have a buffet service, a billiard room and a bowling green. We are out in the open countryside, and the green of the meadows and the fresh air make us feel more open and eager to confide in one another. Our owner too comes to spend a few hours among his workmen, after an exhausting day spent in the factory.

In front of us, the oleo-dynamic working presses.

THE ‘GAMINE’ IDEA

You may be interested in this story, and we are happy to satisfy you. One day, Comm. Vignale thought of meeting young people’s wishes with a small car, thirties style, but seen through the eyes of the present time. The idea seemed a good one, and the necessary chassis could only be that of the Fiat 500. The coachbuilder immediately got down to work, and moved so fast that within a few weeks the prototype had taken shape and was ready for testing on the road. That car was given the name ‘Gamine’, which in French means ‘little scamp’.

The first prototype was sent to France, where it was very successful. Now the ‘Gamine’ is regularly produced, and after being sold all over Italy, is about to cross the ocean to reach the United States. If you think that Mr. Vignale started his work with just a 500, this nostalgic return to that era could not have been celebrated with better luck.

There are many people, not exactly young any more, who are interested in the ‘Gamine’ — but that is another story.

Right here is where the little Gamine is born…

A GIGANTIC TROPHY

It seems unbelievable that such a little car could make people talk about it so much, but it happens. Since its first appearance at the Paris exhibition, the public has awarded it success. Little by little it has grown in importance, and you can see it everywhere: it takes part in competitions and is awarded first prize, it even competes in rallies and wins trophies bigger than itself. At the last Monaco Grand Prix, it even opened the competitors’ line-up and was photographed near Prince Rainier.

Just so you know, this little car won the Journalistic Montecarlo Rally 1968, receiving the first prize prototype trophy, driven by M. Bozon (Équipe) and M. Issontier (Dépêche de Toulouse).

The Gamine has even opened the competitors’ line during the last Monaco Grand Prix.

A COAT FOR WINTER

The little Gamine, of course, could not always go around in a summer dress, so a little coat has been prepared for it. Vignale took this problem to heart, and we can be sure that, once dressed in this way, the Gamine will face the rigours of winter without any fear.

You have certainly understood that the car will be fitted with a ‘Hard Top’, which will give the little car a dignified and comfortable air. A new edition of this Fiat 500, which young people long for and older people envy, will be finished very soon.

It seems, in fact, to be a kind of spatial complex…
